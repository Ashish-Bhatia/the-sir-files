India is not one denominator.

The biggest figures in the SIR debate tend to carry the least explanation. We need to separate a missing draft entry, a final-roll deletion, an appeal and a later restoration before counting what changed.

What would the number measure?

An allegation that millions “disappeared” can describe several different realities. Someone may not return an enumeration form, be missing from a draft, appear on a different constituency’s roll, be formally deleted after an ERO decision, or return through a later accepted application. Those categories should not be added or treated as synonyms.

There is no single authenticated national person-level dataset in this archive from which to calculate the total number of eligible voters who were finally excluded contrary to law. That is an explicit limit—not a reason to accept either an allegation or a denial without inspection.

Goa: what a complete reconciliation looks like

Goa’s signed press note shows that good national research is possible, provided the stages are respected. It separates 1,00,042 initial draft non-inclusions from the further decisions and application outcomes between draft and final.

Bihar: a different stage, a different question

Approximately 7.89 crore entries preceded Bihar’s exercise, 7.24 crore appeared on the draft and 7.42 crore on the final roll. About 21.53 lakh additions and 3.66 lakh further removals were recorded after the draft. That final net decline is not interchangeable with the number initially omitted from the draft.

A meaningful audit follows each elector’s old and new entry, the reason for any change, the competent officer’s decision and appeals. Without that, even an accurate state-wide arithmetic total cannot determine how many exclusions were erroneous.

The 19-jurisdiction May order

Unlike a national simultaneous exercise, the May order used multiple timetable groups. Some original final dates fell in September, while Tripura’s was December. Later directions modified some timetables. A national table that combines draft figures in one state with final figures in another does not measure a consistent point in the process.

Five tests every national claim must pass

  1. Use the same revision stage in all jurisdictions.
  2. Separate changes of location and duplication from loss of registration.
  3. Keep submitted applications distinct from accepted decisions.
  4. Include reversals and post-publication restoration without double counting.
  5. Distinguish proven wrongful exclusions from all administrative decreases.

The aim is neither to establish a favourable total nor to produce the largest number. It is to publish a result that someone else can reproduce from the records.
