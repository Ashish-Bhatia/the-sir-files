FORMS & LAW

How the process works.

The officials, forms and decisions you meet when reading about SIR.

From fieldwork to the final list

Booth Level Officers collect and check information. A draft roll is published. People submit claims and objections. The Electoral Registration Officer makes the registration decisions. A final roll follows, with appeals and later updating subject to the applicable rules and election-period restrictions.

Form 6: inclusion

Form 6 is the application for inclusion. The dispute covered here concerns how its first-time declaration applies to previously registered people returning after SIR. A completed form is an application, not proof that inclusion has occurred.

Read about the form wording · Read the Delhi order

Form 7: objections and deletion requests

An objection must be distinguished from the decision that follows it. The October directions set individual application limits and scrutiny safeguards. Party-appointed agents have separate rules.

Read the Form 7 directions

Form 8: changes to an existing entry

The Delhi petition sought correction and shifting through Form 8 rather than fresh inclusion through Form 6. The route directed in a particular case must be read from its applicable instructions and order.

The legal framework

The Representation of the People Act, 1950 and Registration of Electors Rules, 1960 govern the process. The July 2025 ECI Act compilation is newer than the historical file previously linked here, but later changes still need checking.

What the courts decided

The May Supreme Court judgment upheld the Bihar framework with legal limits. West Bengal orders gave state-specific procedural directions. The Delhi October order addressed two applicants. These are different decisions with different scopes.

ECINet and digital checks

Software records and discrepancy flags should be traced to the notice, decision and roll change they produced. An announced independent review is different from a published report.
