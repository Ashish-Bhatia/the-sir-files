Karnataka

Bulk Form 7 applications are a legitimate object of forensic examination. Their existence alone does not prove that voters were deleted or that crimes were committed.

Karnataka presents a test of two systems operating in parallel: electoral adjudication and criminal investigation. In October 2026, proceedings concerning allegedly false Form 7 objections were reported in the High Court. The central evidence is the original application, applicant authentication, the objected-to elector’s response, and what the ERO actually decided.

Applications are not deletions

An applicant may claim that a registered person died, shifted, duplicated an entry or lacked eligibility. The ERO must consider the statutory ground and the evidence. An unproved assertion in Form 7 should not be counted as a completed deletion.

A historical warning

The Commission’s account of the Aland matter described 6,018 applications in 2023, of which 24 were considered genuine. That historical incident is an important comparator—but not proof about people involved in 2026 litigation.

Electronic provenance matters

Preservation should reach original forms, submission timestamps, authentication events and case histories. A headline about a stay or alleged impersonation is not a signed criminal judgment.

What is still unknown

  • Can authenticated High Court orders and case numbers be obtained?
  • How many disputed objections were accepted, rejected or left pending?
  • What do original electronic submission records show?
The published records do not establish whether every individual decision was correct.
