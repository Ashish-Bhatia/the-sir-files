Last desk review: 15 October 2026

Judicial safeguards in practice

In West Bengal, the question moved from whether a revision could happen to whether affected electors could understand and challenge discrepancies. Supreme Court orders in January and February 2026 addressed notice accessibility and the adjudication of pending cases, including an exceptional role for judicial officers.

Notice that can be acted upon

A notice is useful only if its grounds are intelligible, it reaches the person concerned, and there is time and a genuine way to respond. The Court’s directions recognised the importance of accessible discrepancy lists and practical opportunities to resolve them.

Exceptional does not mean universal

The February directions must be understood in their West Bengal context. They should not be described as transferring the ERO’s normal legal duties to courts throughout India.

The missing denominator

Without full sets of notices, decisions, successful challenges and appeals, it is impossible to measure accurately how many eligible electors were finally and wrongly excluded.

What this file still needs

  1. What was the actual backlog when exceptional adjudication began?
  2. How many disputed notices resulted in retention, correction or exclusion?
  3. Were orders implemented in the operative roll before election deadlines?
