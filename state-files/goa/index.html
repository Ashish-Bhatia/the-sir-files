Last desk review: 15 October 2026

The most complete numerical record

The Goa case is not principally about one dramatic number. It is about whether each part of the roll can be explained. The state’s signed final-publication report separates draft omissions, accepted additions, form-based deletions and determinations made after discrepancy proceedings. It provides the clearest opportunity yet to connect software flags to accountable decisions.

A reconciliation that balances

Starting from the draft, 12,166 accepted additions and 39,592 removals produce the reported final electorate exactly. Of those removals, 3,812 were processed through forms, while 35,780 followed eligibility proceedings relating to unmatched records or logical discrepancies.

The flags behind the notices

ECINet reportedly flagged 58,923 logical discrepancies among historically linked voters; 41,116 involved a father’s-name mismatch. Names can differ through spelling, transliteration or source error. A flag is a request for examination, not proof that an elector is ineligible.

The unfinished 97-person file

ECI subsequently identified 97 people requiring special follow-up, of whom 81 had submitted Form 6 by 26 September. Goa announced further camps in October. Submitted applications cannot be equated with approved restoration without the final orders and supplements.

The arithmetic, exposed

The CEO’s signed report can be recomputed without estimating any value. The document describes 39,592 removals after draft publication; 3,812 came through processed applications and 35,780 through eligibility determinations. This numerical transparency helps—but it cannot substitute for the reasons in individual orders.

What this file still needs

  1. What statutory grounds supported each of the 35,780 post-draft eligibility decisions?
  2. Of the 97 persons, how many are now enrolled and by which process?
  3. Which ECINet matching-rule versions were used, and could authorised ERO decisions be implemented?
