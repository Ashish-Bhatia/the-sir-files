A new stage, not a new label for SIR

The signed 15 October order schedules SSR in 17 jurisdictions, defers it in 16 and states SIR has not started in Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh. Its SSR timetable is draft 12 November, claims 12 November–11 December, disposal by 28 December and final publication 4 January 2027, against the 1 January qualifying date. Order, page 1

The scheduled jurisdictions are Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Goa, Gujarat, Kerala, Lakshadweep, Madhya Pradesh, Manipur, Puducherry, Punjab, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and West Bengal.

Scheduling does not independently establish that any earlier SIR outcome was correct. For analytics, retain a separate version and effective date for every roll and supplement. Do not overwrite a SIR final snapshot with an SSR draft.

Build a longitudinal reconciliation

Start with one frozen pre-revision cohort. Assign a protected research key and retain each original roll reference. Link later entries only when the evidence supports identity continuity. Polling-part reorganisation and serial-number changes require explicit mapping, so an apparent disappearance is not resolved by string matching alone.

Define the measures before requesting data

  • Contact coverage: unique cohort members reached divided by unique assigned cohort members.
  • Application coverage: unique eligible omitted cohort members applying, with the eligibility denominator explicitly documented.
  • Decision completion: disposed applications divided by received applications in a matched period.
  • Publication completion: accepted cases reflected in the operative roll divided by accepted cases due for publication.
  • Timely restoration: eligible earlier omissions restored before the relevant election cutoff.

Report unknown eligibility, uncertain identity links and pending appeals rather than silently dropping them. Publish the counts behind each fraction. A percentage without the missing-data count invites a false comparison.

Test workflow capacity without inventing a backlog

Obtain assigned workload, active field staff, achieved weekly coverage and case ageing. Then compare measured workload with achieved throughput. Do not infer non-compliance from the suggested weekly coverage alone, and do not treat visits as completed applications or accepted applications as published entries.

For genuine bottleneck analysis, distinguish contact failure, missing documents, statutory inquiry, a decision queue, an approved transfer, publication delay and election-period restrictions. The same elapsed time might contain several of these stages.

The key unresolved question

How many eligible people omitted earlier were back on the operative roll by the time they next had an opportunity to vote? Answering it needs dated case and publication records. New revision schedules create an opportunity to test that question, but they do not answer it.

What the 15 October SSR order requires

These directions apply to the 17 scheduled SSR jurisdictions. They are not proof of compliance, nor an automatic replacement timetable for Delhi.

Before a decision

Paragraph 13 sets display and waiting-period requirements, including seven clear days after the required publication and sharing with political parties. It also requires personal notice for proposed deletions other than death cases and an opportunity to be heard where the stated procedure applies.

Personal cross-checks

Paragraph 20(b) requires the ERO personally to cross-check cases where deletions exceed 2% of a polling station’s electors, or the same person objects in more than five cases. These are checking triggers. They do not authorise a quota of deletions or replace the individual filing limits.

Readable copies

Paragraph 23(c)(ii) requires a free hard copy and readable PDF, without elector photographs, for recognised parties after draft and final publication. It does not promise an unrestricted public CSV or API.

Appeals

Paragraph 19 identifies the District Magistrate as first appellate authority and the CEO as second, under section 24 and rule 27. A particular appeal still needs the applicable instructions and order/service dates.
