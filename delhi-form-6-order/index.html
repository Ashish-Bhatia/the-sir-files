EXPLAINER / 17 OCTOBER 2026

Returning to the roll: what the Delhi order decides.

A four-page court order answers a question for two applicants. It does not establish their eventual inclusion or settle the wider Form 6 dispute.

The decision, in context

In Nilotpal Datta & Anr. v. ECI, W.P.(C) 14955/2026, the Delhi High Court closed proceedings on 14 October. Paragraph 7 permits the petitioners to file Form 6 for processing under law. Paragraph 6 rejects the first petitioner’s concern about a false-declaration accusation in light of the ECI counsel’s position, given on the ERO’s instructions. Order, paragraphs 6–7

What this leaves open

The order does not record completed inclusion. It does not supply a revised form or decide every issue raised about the declaration. Our reading is therefore narrow: an application route is recorded, while the result still needs evidence.

Follow the application through

For an outcome audit, we would need the receipt, the ERO’s decision and the corresponding entry in a published roll or supplement. A statement about the availability of a form cannot replace those records.

A separate administrative development

The Indian Express reports a Delhi re-verification drive covering roughly 47.5 lakh draft omissions. This is reported activity. We have not inspected the signed local instruction or matched restoration results. 17 October report

How we checked the document

We read the four-page publisher-hosted PDF and inspected pages 3–4 visually. A second publisher supplied identical bytes. That is a copy check, not independent corroboration. Court-portal and cryptographic-signature verification remain outstanding. The source ledger preserves the file fingerprint.
