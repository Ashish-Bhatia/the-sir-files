STATE FILE / DETAILED FILE

West Bengal

The West Bengal proceedings illustrate how the courts can order exceptional procedures without rewriting the system for the entire country.

First key order, 202619 Jan
Further directions, 202620 Feb
Individual-level outcomesOpen

In West Bengal, the question moved from whether a revision could happen to whether affected electors could understand and challenge discrepancies. Supreme Court orders in January and February 2026 addressed notice accessibility and the adjudication of pending cases, including an exceptional role for judicial officers.

What is established here

SSR scheduled in the 15 October order. ECI-SSR-OCT15

Historical reference: 01 Jan 2002 / 01 Jan 2026. ECI-2026-05

Notice that can be acted upon

The January order directs accessible lists, authorised representatives and additional time linked to list display. These are judicial safeguards; implementation still needs dated registers and receipt records.

Exceptional does not mean universal

The February order invokes Article 142 for an extraordinary West Bengal arrangement involving judicial officers. Its paragraph 9 also permits an ongoing process and later supplementary lists. This is not a universal replacement of ERO duties.

The missing denominator

Without full sets of notices, decisions, successful challenges and appeals, it is impossible to measure accurately how many eligible electors were finally and wrongly excluded.

Evidence still needed

  • What was the actual backlog when exceptional adjudication began?
  • How many disputed notices resulted in retention, correction or exclusion?
  • Were orders implemented in the operative roll before election deadlines?
