RESEARCH REVIEW / 17 OCTOBER 2026

Audit the path from decision to roll.

A forensic work plan for ECINet, historical matching and statutory decision-making. The procedures below are proposed tests, not findings about undisclosed software.

What is established

Goa’s official report records 58,923 logical-discrepancy flags and 182,403 unmapped electors. It separately reports 35,780 ineligibility determinations after processing the two categories. It does not supply a cross-tab showing how many final adverse decisions came from each group. GOA-FINAL

The September government account announces expert review and describes role-based access. Announcement of a review does not establish its completion, independence in practice or findings. NAG-SEP26

1. Preserve a reproducible evidence set

Obtain authorised, dated extracts of source rolls, OCR output, normalised records, match candidates, flag results, notices, documents, hearing outcomes, signed decisions, publication batches and supplements. Hash files, retain the original bytes and record time zone, source, custodian, export filters and access limits. Keep sensitive records under controlled access and publish aggregate results.

Retention and access should be documented for each requested record. A missing log is a visibility limitation. It becomes a proven retention breach only after establishing the applicable requirement and the facts of destruction or non-retention.

2. Reconstruct each state’s mapping

Use explicit state, constituency, part and roll-version keys. Historical constituency boundaries, reserialisation and changing part numbers must be mapped before comparing records. Separate strong identity links from uncertain fuzzy matches. A low match score must not silently become an adverse legal finding.

Build test sets containing spelling variants, transliteration differences, changed surnames, missing ages, notional addresses and inconsistent parent relationships. Measure false flags against human-verified reference cases. Report uncertainty in the reference labels themselves.

3. Test rules, not inferred model architecture

Request rule versions, threshold changes, release approvals and effective dates. Re-run the same authorised test records under each version. Record which changes affect only a review queue and which affect the ability to decide or publish. Do not describe a rule engine as AI or claim access to proprietary internals without evidence.

5. Test alternative explanations

For an apparent blocked enrolment, test incomplete documentation, a pending statutory decision, an election-period freeze, an approved transfer, a queue delay, duplicate resolution and an unauthorised technical restriction. Preserve timestamps and written reasons. More than one explanation might apply, so avoid coding every delay as deliberate interference.

6. Measure harm at the correct date

Publish both process metrics and registration outcomes. The useful sequence is: identified, contacted, applied, accepted, published, and eligible to vote at the relevant election. A restoration after polling does not erase an earlier exclusion, but the earlier exclusion must itself be evidenced.

Assess disparity only with lawful and reliable demographic data. Do not infer religion or citizenship from voter names. Report rates with denominators, sample weights, confidence intervals and missing-data sensitivity. Differences identify questions for causal investigation.

7. Publish an audit trail readers can challenge

Release the analysis code, a redacted data dictionary, sampling rules, aggregate results and documented corrections. Give the responsible institutions a fair opportunity to respond before a case-level accusation is treated as established. Record responses and unresolved contradictions separately. This edition has not commissioned an audit, accessed internal systems or sent records requests.
