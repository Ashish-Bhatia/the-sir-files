CONSTITUTION / STATUTE / PRACTICE

The power to revise.
The duty to decide fairly.

The Supreme Court has upheld the Commission’s authority to conduct special intensive revision. That settles a constitutional issue. It does not remove the statutory duties attached to each application, objection or exclusion.

The Commission’s power

Article 324 places superintendence and control of electoral-roll preparation with the Election Commission. Section 21(3) of the Representation of the People Act, 1950 provides for special revision with recorded reasons. Rule 25 sets out the intensive-revision pathway.

In Association for Democratic Reforms v ECI, decided on 27 May 2026, the Supreme Court upheld Bihar’s SIR under that framework. This does not confer unlimited power to disregard the registration rules, nor does it settle each later state’s individual disputes.

Who is entitled to be on the roll?

Statutory entitlement depends on Indian citizenship, the relevant qualifying age, ordinary residence and disqualifications as defined by law. Historic rolls and identity documents can help establish facts. A failed software match is not a self-executing legal determination of ineligibility.

A BLO is not an ERO

Booth Level Officers undertake field verification and report probable facts. Assistant Electoral Registration Officers and Electoral Registration Officers perform registration functions according to delegated authority. The person who visits a house should not be confused with the authority issuing a statutory decision.

Original ECI instructions require additional review where BLO recommendations are adverse; the adequacy of any case turns on the applicable phase instructions and the actual decision file.

The right to an effective answer

Draft publication opens a claims-and-objections process. Rules 17–22 regulate scrutiny, notice and inquiry in defined circumstances. The May 2026 Supreme Court judgment did not mandate the same notice at every preliminary step. It did affirm that the governing safeguards must operate before adverse final decisions.

Appeal routes and election deadlines

Section 24 of the 1950 Act and Rule 27 provide the ordinary two-tier appellate route described in Goa’s official press note, with specified time limits. Section 23(3) also bars the specified Section 22 amendments, transpositions and deletions, and Section 23 inclusion directions, after the last nomination date and before completion of the relevant election. That is why a voter restored after an election may still have suffered a lost opportunity to vote earlier.

Citizenship and the limits of ERO powers

The Supreme Court distinguished ERO eligibility inquiries from formal decisions on citizenship. The Citizenship Act’s birth-based rules change across dates. Neither an old-roll absence nor a checklist can replace a reasoned application of the statute to a person’s actual circumstances.

A live form dispute

The modified Form 6 declaration used during SIR presents an interpretive issue where previously registered electors are instructed to seek inclusion again. Contemporaneous October reporting attributes clarification to the Court, but this archive does not substitute press reporting for a signed judgment or order. The legality of the precise form modification remains marked unresolved in this review.

Reader guidance, not legal advice: If a person’s entry is absent, the immediate action is to check the operative electoral roll and appropriate ERO channel, preserve application acknowledgements and use the valid statutory remedies. A photo identity card alone does not prove that an entry appears on the current roll.
