ARCHIVE STATUSActive

Last desk review: 15 October 2026

The case that reached the Supreme Court

A contested enumeration campaign in 2025 changed the language of India’s electoral-roll debate. Its draft was missing roughly 65 lakh entries from the pre-revision electorate. Months later, the Supreme Court upheld the Commission’s authority and the framework used. Neither event, alone, tells us whether every omitted citizen had a fair chance to remain on the roll.

What the Court decided

In May 2026 the Supreme Court held that Article 324 and Section 21(3) allow special intensive revision. The Court accepted the central aim of correcting the register and found the Bihar framework compliant with the governing election rules. It maintained a boundary: an ERO’s inquiry into eligibility must not be mistaken for formal adjudication of Indian citizenship.

What the numbers leave unanswered

A draft name not found and a final deletion are different events. The official figures record approximately 21.53 lakh additions and 3.66 lakh further removals between draft and final. Without identity-level links, a simple subtraction cannot identify how many people from the original missing cohort later returned.

Where scrutiny belongs

For any challenged case, the source is a specific ERO order: the alleged ground, BLO inquiries, the notice and the hearing record. A system-wide judgment cannot substitute for inspecting those files.

What this file still needs

  1. How many of the draft’s 65 lakh absent names reappeared through Form 6 or corrections?
  2. What evidence supported the combined “shifted/not found” classification?
  3. Were appeals and citizenship referrals resolved within the prescribed periods?
