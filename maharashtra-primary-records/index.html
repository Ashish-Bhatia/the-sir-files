CONTINUATION REVIEW / 17 OCTOBER 2026

Maharashtra: the primary records narrow the questions.

A signed extension replaces the earlier reporting-only schedule. Application intake remains distinct from accepted claims and actual roll entries.

What changed in this review

The 12 October ECI letter and attached notification set claims through 26 October, disposal through 10 November, and final publication for 16 November. The letter directs State Gazette publication; we have not located the actual Gazette issue. Signed letter and notification, pages 1–2

That closes the source-availability gap for the extension itself. It leaves separate questions about publication, public communication and implementation. Neither the presence nor absence of the Gazette issue in this archive alone establishes a legal breach.

The CEO bulletin measures intake

The Marathi CEO bulletin dated 9 October records applications from draft publication through 8 October:

FormApplicationsDo not treat as

The bulletin addresses new and otherwise eligible omitted electors together. It also describes an “Any other Documents” upload option for notice responses. It does not publish acceptance totals. Its 12 October claims deadline predates the later extension. Official bulletin, visually inspected

The three form counts total 1,138,055 applications. This calculated sum measures form activity. It does not identify 1,138,055 distinct people. A person might appear in more than one form stream, and a form might be rejected, withdrawn or still pending.

A denominator problem, not merely a missing column

A restoration rate needs the same cohort in numerator and denominator. Using all Form 6 applications as the numerator counts new voters alongside previously omitted people. Using all provisional omissions as the denominator also includes cases ultimately found ineligible or registered elsewhere. Even matching the dates would not repair those population differences.

Request a cohort split before computing a percentage: previously enrolled and omitted, newly eligible, transfer or other inclusion. For each cohort, track received, accepted, rejected, withdrawn, pending and published outcomes. Reconcile applications to people under controlled access, then publish aggregate tables.

The public timetable is internally inconsistent

At inspection, Pune’s official SIR page showed an overview with an October final target and a separate Important Dates table with a November final target. The same page linked the later signed extension. These are inconsistent public instructions, not proof that a particular voter was harmed. PUNE-SIR-PAGE

The remedy to investigate is specific: which sections were visible on each date, when the newer notice was linked, what outreach occurred and whether an affected applicant relied on an older section. Website footer dates cannot establish the version history of every paragraph.

Do not merge separate court events

Bar & Bench reported the Maharashtra plea was proposed for hearing on 26 October and immediate notice was declined. This differs from reporting about notice in another SIR petition. We have not authenticated the signed Maharashtra order or docket identity. BB-MH-OCT15

In the court register, use one record per case and procedural event. A hearing date, notice, interim direction and final holding belong in separate fields. Allegations about discrepancies remain allegations until adjudicated or independently established.

Evidence requests, now narrowed

  1. Actual Gazette publication of the signed extension.
  2. Form-wise and cohort-wise disposal returns matching the bulletin cutoff.
  3. Accepted-to-published reconciliation, with rejected and pending cases preserved.
  4. Public-page version history and a documented correction trail.
  5. The authenticated Maharashtra court order and complete case identifiers.

These are research targets. No request or court filing has been submitted by this publication.
